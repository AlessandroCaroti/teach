# EXAM.md Format

`EXAM.md` lives at the workspace root. It captures what the student is being tested on, when, and how — the frame that every study decision should trace back to.

## Template

```md
# Exam: {Subject / Course / Certification}

## What it is
{1-3 sentences: the exam itself — course code, certifying body, level. What kind of exam is it (final, midterm, licensing, entrance)?}

## When
{Date, or how much time is available if no fixed date. This drives pacing in "What to Study Next".}

## Format
{Written / oral / multiple-choice / practical / open-book / closed-book / timed (how long) / mixed. Number and type of questions if known.}

## Why this matters
{Optional, one sentence. Stakes and context — required to graduate, a career-change certification, a low-stakes quiz — enough to calibrate tone and depth, not a motivational essay.}

## Target
{What success looks like: a passing threshold, a target grade, or just "pass".}

## Constraints
{Study time actually available, prior commitments, anything that bounds how much can realistically be covered.}

## Out of scope
{Topics the materials don't cover, or the student or instructor has explicitly excluded.}
```

## Rules

- **One exam per workspace.** Studying for two unrelated exams is two workspaces.
- **Concrete over vague.** "Pass the CFA Level I in November" beats "get better at finance."
- **Don't invent what you don't know.** If the student hasn't told you the format or date, leave it open and ask. A wrong assumption here misdirects everything downstream.
- **Revise when reality shifts.** Exam dates move, scope narrows, formats change. Update this file and confirm with the student rather than assuming.
- **Keep it short.** This is a compass, not a study plan. If `EXAM.md` runs past a screen, the detail belongs in `SYLLABUS.md` instead.
- **Take "Out of scope" as seriously as the syllabus itself.** It's how this file protects the student's study time — see Source of Truth in [SKILL.md](./SKILL.md).
